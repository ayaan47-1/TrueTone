Preferences

Making room for your preferences.

Start with what you like.

A routine can begin with simple preferences. Perhaps you enjoy a light texture, prefer a fragrance-free product, or want fewer steps on a busy morning. Those choices are useful starting points.

Write a short list.

Note the categories you already use, the textures you enjoy and the budget you want to stay within. Keep observations about appearance separate from questions about skin health.

Leave room to change your mind.

Preferences can change with your schedule. A shorter routine may suit one day and a longer one another. Follow product labels, and seek a dermatologist’s advice for anything concerning on your skin.

The details

A closer look at the label.

Read beyond the name.

Two products in the same category can offer different textures, finishes and directions. Read the product description alongside the label rather than relying on the name alone.

Keep the details together.

Application instructions, ingredient information and storage directions belong in the same decision. Follow the label for the product you choose. Do not assume that a catalog entry independently verifies a product’s claims.

Know what a preference can tell you.

A preference for a particular texture or finish is not a skin-health assessment. TrueTone’s routine experience is built around stated preferences. A selfie doesn’t determine skincare needs.

Daily habits

A space for the everyday.

Give the familiar things a place.

A small tray or shelf can make it easier to find the objects you use regularly. Arrange them in a way that makes sense to you; there is no required collection to own.

Let the label guide storage.

Check the storage directions on your products before choosing where they live. Keep labels readable and lids with their containers.

Keep a useful note.

A short list of the textures, fragrances and finishes you enjoy can be more helpful than adding another step. Your routine is a set of choices, not a score to improve.
